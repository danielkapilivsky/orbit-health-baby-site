Health Disclaimer

Last updated October 29, 2026

Please read this before relying on any number in Orbit Health Baby.

Guidelines, not medical advice

The daily targets and limits in Orbit Health Baby are general guidelines for pregnancy, based on your age and trimester. They are not a prescription. Your doctor or midwife may recommend different amounts for you, for example if you are expecting more than one baby or have a health condition. Always follow their advice.

Orbit Health Baby does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any condition, and it is not a medical device.

Talk to your doctor or midwife

Ask your doctor or midwife about your diet, your prenatal vitamin and any other supplements or medication. Do not start, stop or change anything you take because of a number in this app.

If something feels wrong

The app cannot tell you whether you or your baby are well. If you have bleeding, severe pain, fever, a change in your baby's movements, or any worry at all, contact your doctor, midwife or local emergency services right away. Do not wait to check the app.

Weight, food and tracking

Healthy weight gain in pregnancy varies a lot from woman to woman, and the app does not tell you what yours should be. Counting food and tracking weight can be stressful or harmful for some people. If tracking makes you anxious, or you have or suspect an eating disorder, stop using the app and talk to a healthcare professional.

Food data and label scanning

Most of the built-in food list comes from the U.S. Department of Agriculture, FoodData Central (SR Legacy). Values are averages for generic foods and can differ from a specific product, so check the label when accuracy matters. Caffeine and water amounts for drinks are typical values.

When you scan a nutrition label, the app reads the text on your phone and fills in a food for you to check. Text recognition can misread numbers, so compare each one with the package before saving.

The app does not tell you which foods are safe to eat in pregnancy and must not be used to manage food allergies. Ask your doctor or midwife.

Baby size and dates

The week of pregnancy comes from the due date you enter, and the fruit and vegetable size comparisons are traditional approximations. Every baby grows at their own pace. Only your healthcare provider can tell you how your baby is growing.

Apple Health data

If you connect Apple Health, the steps and Activity rings shown come from Apple Health and your devices. They can be incomplete or inaccurate, and Orbit Health Baby does not verify them. They are for general information only. Ask your doctor or midwife what activity is right for you.
